Chapter 1 · The Obsession

A Nation Hooked on Coffee

Australia's love affair with coffee is decades in the making. Once a tea-drinking nation shaped by its British heritage, Australia underwent a quiet revolution in the 1960s when Italian and Greek immigrants brought espresso culture to Melbourne's cafes. Today, 75% of Australian adults drink at least one cup a day. Even a global pandemic couldn't break the habit; it simply moved it indoors, with home consumption surging 37% in 2020.

Chapter 2 · The Landscape

A Cafe on Every Corner — Almost

With over 55,000 cafes and restaurants operating in 2024, Australia's coffee shop landscape is dense and fiercely independent — 95% of all coffee establishments are independently owned. New South Wales and Victoria together account for more than half of all establishments, while Melbourne has earned a global reputation as a coffee capital.

Chapter 3 · The Cup

Latte, Flat White, or Cappuccino?

Ask an Australian their coffee order and you'll get a highly specific answer. Unlike many countries where black coffee dominates, Australians overwhelmingly prefer milk-based espresso drinks. The cappuccino leads nationally, though the latte rules the cafe counter by sales volume. The flat white — Australia's own invention — sits in third place, beloved by those who want their coffee to taste like coffee.

The flat white debate: The flat white was invented in either Australia or New Zealand — both countries still argue about it. It differs from a latte by using a ristretto (shorter, more concentrated) shot and a thinner layer of steamed milk, producing a stronger, more coffee-forward flavour. It has since been adopted by Starbucks worldwide, cementing its status as one of Australia's most significant cultural exports.

Chapter 4 · The Cost

What We Pay for Our Daily Fix

Coffee in Australia is not cheap. The average flat white costs around $5.50 and the average Australian coffee drinker spends roughly $1,268 per year on cafe coffee alone. Despite cost-of-living pressures squeezing household budgets in 2023–24, coffee spending proved remarkably resilient. Prices vary considerably by city, with Sydney consistently topping the charts.

Chapter 5 · The Origins

Australia's Coffee Comes From Around the World

Australia produces only about 600 tonnes of coffee domestically — a fraction of its annual consumption of nearly 126,000 tonnes. Brazil and Colombia supply the bulk of Australia's green Arabica beans. Vietnam's Robusta fuels the instant coffee market, while Ethiopia, Guatemala, and Papua New Guinea supply the growing specialty segment. In 2024, a 50-year peak in global green coffee prices sent import costs surging 77%.

Chapter 7 · The Home Brew

Bringing the Cafe Home

COVID-19 accelerated a trend already quietly underway: Australians bringing barista-quality coffee into their own kitchens. Home consumption surged 37% during the pandemic. Today, 65% of Australian households own a coffee machine, and pod machines now account for 41% of all coffee consumed at home. Chapter 8 · The Industry

A $2.4 Billion Industry — and Growing

The Australian coffee market was valued at USD 2.44 billion in 2025 and is projected to reach USD 3.37 billion by 2031, growing at a compound annual rate of 5.55%. The fastest-growing segment is ready-to-drink (RTD) canned coffee — growing at 11.4% annually — driven by Gen Z convenience preferences. Specialty coffee is next at 7.7% CAGR, while instant coffee — still the largest segment by volume — is growing slowly as tastes sophisticate.

Chapter 9 · What's Next

Where Is the Cup Going?

The next chapter of Australian coffee is being written by specialty roasters, cold brew innovators, and a generation of Gen Z consumers as likely to order a nitro cold brew as a cappuccino. Climate change threatens coffee-growing regions worldwide, and 2024's 50-year price peak may be a preview of what's to come. Whatever happens, Australians will find a way to get their coffee.

The market is set to reach $3.37 billion by 2031

Solid line = historical data. Dashed line = projected growth (CAGR 5.55%). Each point is a year milestone.

Figure 19 — Australian coffee market value (USD billions), historical 2020–2025 and projected 2026–2031. Source: Mordor Intelligence, Australian Coffee Market Report 2025–2031.

Looking ahead: Specialty coffees (organic, single-origin, direct-trade) are growing at a CAGR of 7.7% and are projected to take an increasingly large slice of the market by 2031 — driven by younger, ethically conscious consumers who treat their coffee purchase as an expression of values. Ready-to-drink formats, meanwhile, are the fastest-growing segment at 11.4% CAGR, set to reshape how Australians consume coffee on the go.

"Whatever the price, whatever the technology, whatever the trend — Australia will not give up its coffee. The question is only what form the next cup will take."
